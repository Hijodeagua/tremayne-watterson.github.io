About

I love using data to understand the world around us.

I would often use stats to argue with my friends about which teams or players were the best, but my professional relationship with data began in earnest with public health. I learned about the Flint water crisis and saw the disparate impacts of poor decision making. I was shocked that this could occur in the best country in the world, and I later learned just how difficult the policy making process was. I wanted to use data to put numbers alongside the stories of the people most at the margins of our society. After earning a biostatistics degree at Saint Louis University, I spent a year with AmeriCorps in Woodburn, Oregon, helping immigrant farmworker families find housing, healthcare and immigration support. When COVID hit, I came home to St. Louis and worked as a contact tracer and STD/HIV case investigator for the Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services. Then I earned my MPH in Epidemiology and Biostatistics at WashU while serving as lead data manager for a 2,000+ participant COVID-19 study at the School of Medicine.

For the last three years, I've built up my technical skills in the fintech sector at Red Ventures. I've owned a dynamic lead pricing model that underpins a system bringing in $100K+ a day, been the sole data scientist for multiple business units, and built successful, long-lasting models from the ground up. In this role, I've found both plenty of parallels and real differences between academia and health care and the tech and business worlds. But regardless of the field, to make analysis trustworthy, you need to build relationships with stakeholders, make smart evidence-based plans, and leave plenty of documentation so others can pick up where you left off, because priorities can change at the drop of a hat or an executive order.

As AI use grows in this field, it's important to ask when and how to use it best. But as a mentor of mine in college once told me, data means nothing if it doesn't lead to changes in real people's lives. That's where I feel my work lies: using the best available tools to make the biggest impact I can.
